### Join Darkbloom's waitlists with `darkbloom waitlist`; sandbox setup waits for approval

- `darkbloom waitlist` lists every waitlist the coordinator offers (`GET /v1/waitlists` on the coordinator of `provider.toml`, with the `darkbloom login` token) and where your account stands on each: not joined, your place in line, approved (with the command that starts the feature) or not approved. The coordinator offers waitlists for clusters and Darkbloom Sandbox; a waitlist it adds later appears without a CLI update, and every line takes the waitlist's title and start command from the coordinator.
- `darkbloom waitlist <name>` shows your place on one waitlist, and `darkbloom waitlist <name> join` joins it (`POST /v1/waitlists/{feature}`). The name is the waitlist's id or its plural, so `darkbloom waitlist clusters join --macs 3` and `darkbloom waitlist sandbox` work; an unknown name lists the waitlists there are and exits 1. A waitlist that asks for Macs gets this Mac's chip and memory and `--macs`, by default the fewest it takes and only inside its range; another gets nothing. `--json` prints the coordinator's answer.
- A first `darkbloom sandbox setup` now checks that Darkbloom approved the account from the Darkbloom Sandbox waitlist before it checks or installs anything. Not on the waitlist: setup offers to join it (`--yes` joins); waitlisted, revoked, not logged in or without a usable answer from the coordinator: setup says so and exits 1. A coordinator without waitlists (404) lets setup go on, and a setup that continues, repairs or updates an installed add-on does not ask.
- The cluster commands are unchanged: `darkbloom waitlist clusters join` records the request, and `darkbloom cluster` does not check it.
