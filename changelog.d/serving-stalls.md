### Fewer GPU stalls while serving

- The proactive MLX buffer-cache sweep no longer flushes between busy engines' steps while admission headroom is healthy. It waits for five seconds of whole-Mac idleness and then flushes without reporting device activity, so deadline quiescence and idle calibration survive it; a request that arrives during that flush is admitted after it instead of starting on a pool being emptied. While engines serve it flushes only when the reusable pool crowds out admission headroom, and a refused engine-owned KV charge or native MiMo media reservation now signals the same shortfall-gated reclaim as request reservations. Load gates drop that pool and resample before evicting a resident model. `DARKBLOOM_TTFT_IDLE_CACHE_SWEEP=0` restores the old sweep; refusals keep signalling while segment retention is on.
- Paged KV pools keep up to an eighth of their slot grant (at most 2 GiB) of free segments across requests and reinstall them for the next admission instead of allocating, zero-filling and fencing new 64 MiB segments. New segments wrap zero-fill-on-demand host pages, so admission no longer drains queued GPU work under the process-wide MLX eval lock. Retained segments are uncommitted (no grant, floor or ledger charge), are dropped on a refused admission or on process memory pressure (any refused charge, a pressure sweep or a macOS memory-pressure event) and return to the allocator after 30 s of engine idleness. They count as reclaimable for cold loads: the provider, standalone and managed Nemotron Super load gates release them before evicting a resident model or refusing, and startup preload releases them before skipping a model. `DARKBLOOM_TTFT_SEGMENT_RETENTION=0` restores release-time trimming and device-filled segments; `DARKBLOOM_TTFT_SEGMENT_HOST_ZEROS=0` keeps retention with device fills.
- `darkbloom start` forwards all three rollbacks to the background provider, so they also reach launchd-managed providers.
