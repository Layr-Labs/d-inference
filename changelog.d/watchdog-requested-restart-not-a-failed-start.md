### A restart you ask for after an update no longer rolls the update back

- Until a new release has run for 10 minutes, three failed starts bring back the previous release and quarantine the new one. A `darkbloom restart` (or `start`, or `stop` and then `start`, or a reboot) in those 10 minutes could count as one: the watchdog, started again by the command, looked at the provider the command had just replaced, saw no heartbeat from it, and counted a failed start, so a Mac restarted three times was rolled back and its release quarantined though nothing had failed. Now the watchdog times a provider's start from when that provider started: a restart you ask for is never a failed start. A crash, or a provider that sends no heartbeat in its first 5 minutes (longer with a raised `startup_preload_timeout_secs`), still counts.
- On a `darkbloom cluster` Mac, a clean stop of the cluster service that its supervisor didn't ask for (`darkbloom cluster` replacing a service started from a terminal, a `kill`) no longer counts as a failed start either. A crash, an exit with an error, a service that stops beating and a release that fails its start check still do.
