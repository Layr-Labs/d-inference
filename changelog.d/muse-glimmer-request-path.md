### Muse Glimmer 30B text on a paged slot

- The provider can load NVIDIA's `Muse-Glimmer-30B-NVFP4` (`model_type` `muse_glimmer`) at its pinned revision as a text model on a paged CBv2 slot. The checkpoint's index and every shard are verified by SHA-256 before load; the vision tower is verified and left unloaded, and no media is advertised. Only the two exact identities (`muse-glimmer-30b`, `nvidia/Muse-Glimmer-30B-NVFP4`) are supported; the type under any other id is not. A contiguous KV selection, the paged kill switch or the crash-loop guard refuses the load instead of falling back.
- Requests use the model's own channels: reasoning arrives in `reasoning_content`, the answer in `content`, and ATEM tool calls in `tool_calls`, validated against the declared tools; framing never reaches the client. `reasoning_effort` maps to the template's reasoning strength (thinking off renders `low`, since the model cannot stop reasoning). Omitted sampling fields take the model card's values (temperature 1.0, top-p 0.95, top-k 64).
- The served chat template is the publisher's with two edits: the date comes from the request, and one call argument is parenthesized so the coordinator's renderer compiles it (no rendered byte changes). It is rendered with Python-compatible `tojson`; its prompt goldens are in `provider-swift/Tests/ProviderCoreTests/Fixtures/MuseGlimmer/prompt-golden.json`. `fixtures/prompt-contract/v1/reasoning_effort_vectors.json` gains the `muse_glimmer` family (446 to 475 cases; existing cases unchanged).
- No drafter is loaded yet, no activation floor or load transient is added, and the model is not registered in the coordinator catalog by this change.
