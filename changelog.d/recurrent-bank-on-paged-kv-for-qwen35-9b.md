### Opt-in: the in-memory recurrent bank on paged KV for Qwen3.5-9B

- With `DARKBLOOM_PREFIX_CACHE_MEMORY=1`, a `Qwen3.5-9B` slot on paged KV now keeps its recurrent checkpoints in the in-memory bank. Before, paged storage hosted that bank only for Nemotron Super, so the opt-in gave this model nothing on its default backend. It stays off by default.
- A slot that hosts the bank serves it in place of its encrypted SSD checkpoints: it writes no checkpoint to disk, restores nothing after a restart, and its SSD cache status reads `disabled` / `config_disabled`. Without the opt-in the slot keeps its SSD checkpoints, as before.
- The bank is carved from the slot's KV grant: a quarter of the grant in 256 MiB steps, at most 4 GiB, and no bank when that is under 1 GiB. `DARKBLOOM_CBV2_HYBRID_PREFIX_BYTES` sets another size.
- Entries stay in RAM under the cache scope of the request that wrote them, as on contiguous storage. A coordinator request that arrives without an authenticated scope is served with caching off, so it neither reads nor feeds the bank. Requests on the Mac's own local endpoint carry no scope and share one set of entries on that Mac, as they share its SSD checkpoints today. `DARKBLOOM_PREFIX_CACHE=0` and `DARKBLOOM_CBV2_HYBRID_PREFIX_CACHE=0` turn it off.
- Only the exact `Qwen3.5-9B` artifact qualifies. Every other model on paged KV, and every model on contiguous KV, behaves as before.
- Measured on one M3 Ultra over the local HTTP endpoint, with prompts of 8,297 to 11,918 tokens and 42 turns per configuration; every follow-up reused its prefix and no request failed. With the opt-in alone, the median follow-up time to first token was 2.24 s against 2.60 s from the SSD checkpoints (1.16x). With the canonical prefill grid and the turn-continuation caller also on, it was 0.57 s (4.6x against today's default configuration). One Mac, one model, one run per configuration.
