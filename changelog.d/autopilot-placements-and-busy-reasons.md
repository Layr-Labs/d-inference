### Autopilot placements and busy reasons

- A cached inventory model loaded by a successful Autopilot operation keeps serving after the operation's control lease ends. The adoption ends when the model leaves memory for any reason, when consent is revoked, Autopilot is disabled or the model is deselected, when its build fails verification, and at restart. Adoption permits serving only; downloads, publication and revision updates still need the ordinary selection or a live lease.
- An operation can hold its loaded model for up to 24 hours (`hold_seconds`); a later operation's hold replaces it. Ordinary cold loads and the idle timer skip a held model, and after the hold expires the idle timeout restarts from the expiry. Cold loads into free memory and other models' idle policy are unchanged. A load that would have to evict a held model fails with the existing capacity refusal, and cold-load capacity no longer counts held weights as reclaimable. Another operation can still unload a held model.
- A `provider_busy` refusal now names its first cause, and Autopilot heartbeats report every current busy reason, when the first began, adopted and held models, and the new capabilities.
- A watchdog acts on transitions that last longer than their bound. An Autopilot operation running for 10 minutes is cancelled and fails as `transition_stalled`. A coordinator preload running for 10 minutes is answered `failed` with `transition_stalled`, and a new request starts afresh. A startup preload stuck on one model for 10 minutes stops before its next model. Preloads are never interrupted, so other requests waiting on the same load are unaffected. Stalled loads, model switches, advertisements, reslices and reconnects are counted and reported, never forcibly cleared.
- Without saved Autopilot consent, nothing changes: no new heartbeat fields, placements or watchdog actions. All new protocol fields are optional, and coordinators that do not know them ignore them.
